## Core Philosophy

**We are not gambling. We are investing in mispriced probabilities.**

### The Three Pillars

1. **The market is wrong more often than you think.** Sportsbooks set lines to balance action, not to predict outcomes. When 80% of the public loads one side, the line reflects crowd psychology — not reality.

2. **Small edges compound.** A 55% win rate on -110 bets produces ~4.5% ROI. Over 50 bets, that's real money. We don't need home runs. We need repeatable edges.

3. **Discipline is the only edge that never regresses.** The strategies below provide statistical edges. But variance will test you. The bettor who sticks to the system through a 1-4 start will outperform the emotional bettor who doubles down after losses.

### Decision Framework

For every potential bet, ask:

- **Is there a quantifiable edge?** (Historical ATS data, metrics mismatch, line value)
- **Is the public on the other side?** (Contrarian value)
- **Does advanced data support this?** (KenPom, efficiency margins, Quad 1 record)
- **Am I reacting to narrative or data?** (If you can't cite the number, skip the bet)

If you can't answer YES to at least 2 of these 4 questions, **pass**.

## Bankroll Management

> **Rule #1:** You cannot bet what you cannot afford to lose. Set aside a fixed tournament bankroll and never add to it.

### Unit Sizing

| Approach | Method | Best For |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **Flat Betting** | 1 unit = 1% of bankroll, every bet | Beginners, emotional discipline |
| **Fractional Kelly** | (BP - Q) / B, then divide by 2-4 | Experienced bettors with accurate probability estimates |
| **Tiered Confidence** | 1u (standard), 2u (high conviction), 3u (max, rare) | Intermediate bettors |

### Practical Rules

- **Divide bankroll into 100 units.** $1,000 bankroll = $10/unit.
- **Never risk more than 3-5% on a single bet.** Max 3 units on your highest-conviction play.
- **Limit to 3-5 bets per day.** Quality over quantity. Sharps don't bet every game.
- **Fractional Kelly (1/4 to 1/2 Kelly) is optimal.**
  - Full Kelly: 1/3 chance of halving your bankroll before doubling it.
  - Half Kelly: 1/9 chance. Much safer.
  - Research shows Kelly divided by 2/3 produces the largest long-term ending bankroll.
- **Adjust units as bankroll changes.** If you start at $1,000 and grow to $1,200, your unit becomes $12.
- **Never chase losses.** A bad day is a bad day. Tomorrow's edges don't care about today's results.

- Top-30 defensive efficiency on KenPom
- High 3-point attempt rate (25+ per game) — creates variance
- Won conference by 2+ games (not a fluky autobid)
- Conference tournament champion from a mid-major with non-conference Quad 1 wins

## Advanced Metrics That Predict Wins

### The KenPom Holy Trinity

| Metric | Championship Correlation | Threshold |
|--------|------------------------|-----------|
| **Adjusted Efficiency Margin (AdjEM)** | 24/24 last champions in top 25 | Top 25 = contender |
| **Adjusted Offensive Efficiency (AdjO)** | 23/24 last champions in top 21 | Top 21 = elite |
| **Adjusted Defensive Efficiency (AdjD)** | 22/24 last champions in top 31 | Top 31 = championship-caliber |

### The Balanced Profile Rule

Teams ranked **top 25 in BOTH offense and defense** go on deep runs. Lopsided teams (elite offense, mediocre defense or vice versa) are upset-prone.

- Since 2012, only 5 Final Four teams ranked outside the top 40 in AdjD.
- Every champion since 2001 was top 57 offensively AND top 44 defensively.

### The Cinderella Profile (Double-Digit Seeds Reaching Sweet 16)

| Metric | Average Rank |
|--------|-------------|
| AdjO | 63rd |
| AdjD | 80th |
| AdjEM | 55th |
| Adjusted Tempo | 168th |

**Key insight:** Cinderellas play SLOW. Average tempo rank of 168th means they control pace, limit possessions, and reduce the sample size for the better team to assert dominance.

### Quad 1 Wins as a Fraud Detector

- **10+ Quad 1 wins** = battle-tested, trust the seed
- **3-4 Quad 1 wins with a high seed** = "fraud" candidate, likely overseeded
- **Mid-majors with 2+ road wins vs. Power Four** = already tournament-tested

### The Trapezoid of Excellence

Nearly every national champion falls within a specific AdjEM vs. Adjusted Tempo range. Teams at extreme ends of tempo (very fast OR very slow) are more upset-vulnerable. Flexibility > speed.

## Public Betting Tendencies & Contrarian Value

### The Public's Track Record

| Year | Public ATS Record | ROI |
|------|------------------|-----|
| 2025 | 27-38 (42%) | -20.4% |
| 2024 | 45-21 (68%) | +30.4% (anomaly year) |
| Average (13 years) | .500 or worse in 8 of 13 seasons | Slightly negative |

### The 80% Rule

**Teams receiving 80%+ of spread bets are 2-11 ATS since 2005.**

Teams receiving 75%+ of spread bets: 31-44-2 ATS. This is the single clearest contrarian signal in March Madness.

### How to Identify Sharp vs. Public Money

| Signal | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 30% of bets, 60% of dollars | Sharp money on the 30% side |
| 70% of bets, 70% of dollars | Genuine public consensus (fade) |
| Line moves toward the less-bet side | Reverse line movement = sharp indicator |

### Over/Under Public Tendencies

- When overs are the public side (51%+): **143-176-4 (45%) to the over** — the public is terrible at picking overs
- When unders are the public side: **77-57-1 (58%) to the under** — the public is decent at identifying unders

### Blue Blood Tax

Duke, Kansas, Kentucky, North Carolina attract disproportionate public money. Books shade lines against them, creating value on the other side. **Always check if you're betting a blue blood BECAUSE of the data or because of the name.**

## Line Movement & Sharp Money

### Reading Line Movement

| Timing | What It Means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Early moves** (within hours of release) | Almost always sharp money |
| **Mid-day moves** | Likely public-driven |
| **Late moves** (near tip-off) | Could be either — context matters |

### Reverse Line Movement (RLM)

When 75% of bets go to Team A but the line moves toward Team B, sharp money is on Team B.

- Historical RLM performance (60%+ on one side + 1+ point RLM): **272-203 (57.3%)** for +42.04 units
- This is one of the most reliable signals in sports betting

### Closing Line Value (CLV)

**Consistently beating the closing line is THE most predictive indicator of long-term profitability.**

- Bets placed 24+ hours before tip: average CLV gain of +1.2%
- Bets placed in final hour: average CLV loss of -0.5%

### Timing Strategy

- **Bet favorites EARLY** — public money will inflate the line as game time approaches
- **Bet underdogs LATE** — public action on the favorite creates late value on the dog
- **Bet totals EARLY** — books are less precise on tournament-specific matchup tempos
- **NEVER chase a line** — if a -4 moved to -6, you missed it. Wait for the next edge.

## Round-by-Round Strategy

### First Four
- **Lean:** Underdogs 24-19 ATS (55.8%)
- **Totals:** Under when total is 139+ (15-9)
- **Bonus:** First Four winners are 42-4 ATS (91.3%) in their next game since 2013

### Round of 64
- **Primary play:** Back 12-seeds ATS against 5-seeds (~57% cover rate)
- **Secondary play:** Back 11-seeds ATS against 6-seeds (62% cover rate for the dog)
- **Totals:** First-half unders, especially in early tip-off games (1 PM ET or earlier)
- **Avoid:** Small favorites (-1 to -3) — they're 48-69-4 ATS (41%) since 2009
- **Edge:** Teams that lost their conference championship game are 46-34-2 ATS (57.5%) in Round 1
- **Fade:** Teams that didn't make their conference tournament championship are 44-64-2 ATS (40.7%)

### Round of 32
- **Fade upset winners:** 14-seeds that upset in Round 1 are 0-10 SU and ATS in Round 2
- **Fade blowout winners:** Teams that won by 20+ in Round of 64 are 49-64-2 ATS in Round of 32
- **Look for:** First Four winners continuing their run (91.3% ATS)

### Sweet 16
- **Primary play:** Double-digit seeds as underdogs: 16-7-1 ATS (69.6%) since 2011
- **Fade:** Favorites of 5+ points: 7-15 ATS (31.8%) since 2017
- **Totals:** Under when a mid-major is involved: 26-14 (65%) since 2010

### Elite Eight
- **THE underdog round.** Best round for dogs in the entire tournament.
- Small favorites (4 pts or fewer): 1-10-1 ATS — catastrophic for bettors
- 1-seeds: 32-25 SU but 23-30-4 ATS (43.4%) since 2001
- **If you can pick the winner:** Outright winners are 61-5-2 ATS (92.4%) since 2006
- **Totals:** THE over round: 87-63-2 (58%) since 2001

### Final Four & Championship
- Championship favorites: 26-12 SU (68%) but **19-19 ATS** — dead even
- Championship under: 21-17 (55.2%)
- The spread is meaningless in the championship game — bet totals or moneylines instead

## Live Betting Strategy

### Where the Edge Lives

Tournament games produce massive emotional swings. A cold shooting stretch in the first 10 minutes can push a team from short favorite to double-digit live underdog — even when the game is structurally close.

### The System

1. **Identify the better KenPom team** pregame
2. **If they fall behind early**, check WHY:
   - Shooting variance (cold from 3, missed free throws)? **BET THEM LIVE.**
   - Structural weakness (getting dominated on boards, turnovers, can't break press)? **PASS.**
3. **Look at the underlying indicators:** Rebounding margin, turnover differential, shot quality, pace control
4. **If the better team is winning the "hidden game" but losing the scoreboard**, the live line is overreacting

### Practical Examples

- Pregame -17.5 favorite trails by 8 at half but is winning rebounding 22-14 and has 4 fewer turnovers → Live line might be -8. Bet them.
- Proven defensive powerhouse trails by 10 but opponent is 8/12 from 3 → Unsustainable shooting. Live bet the dog at improved odds.

### Caution

Same bankroll rules apply. Don't over-leverage on live bets. The same discipline that governs pregame bets governs in-game bets.

## Recency Bias & Traps to Avoid

### The Bounce-Back Effect

Teams that **underachieved** the previous tournament **overperform** the following year at rates far exceeding public pick rates.

- Virginia (2019): Lost to 16-seed UMBC in 2018 → Won the championship
- Villanova (2016, 2018): Bounced back from early exits to win titles
- Duke (2015): Won title after prior-year disappointment

**Actionable:** Target last year's early-exit favorites as value picks.

### The Conference Tournament Darling Trap

Conference tournament champions get **overvalued by the public**. The energy and narrative of a dramatic conference tournament run inflates their perceived ability. Oddsmakers know this.

### The Defending Champion Trap

The public overweights last tournament's results. The defending champion will be among the most popular picks regardless of this year's profile. Example: Florida (2026) — defending champion but Todd Golden is 3-5 ATS in March.

### The NIL/Transfer "Upsets Are Dying" Myth

The bracket has been chalky recently (2024 especially). But there is **zero historical correlation** between regular-season upset rates and tournament upset rates. Variance will return.

### Injury Blind Spots

- Injuries to **non-star players** are often NOT fully priced into lines — this creates edges
- Star player injuries are overreacted to by the public, sometimes creating value on the injured team if the line moves too far

### Tier 1: Highest-Conviction Edges (2-3 units)

1. **Back 12-seeds ATS vs. 5-seeds** — 57% cover rate, the single most reliable trend

6. **Back 11-seeds ATS vs. 6-seeds** — 62% dog cover rate
7. **Fade Round of 32 blowout winners** — 49-64-2 ATS after winning by 20+
8. **Elite Eight overs** — 87-63-2 (58%) since 2001
9. **Back coaches with elite ATS records** (Hurley, Enfield, Painter)
10. **Fade coaches with terrible ATS records** (Barnes, Dixon, Lloyd)

### Tier 3: Supporting Edges (1 unit)

12. **Reverse line movement plays** — 57.3% historically
13. **ACC teams as pick-em or small dogs** — 79.2% ATS
14. **Fade SEC teams as low seeds** — 41.2% ATS
15. **Back teams playing in home state** — 80%+ SU since 2002
16. **Championship game under** — 55.2%
17. **Bounce-back teams** from prior year's disappointing exit

### Rules to Never Break

- **Never bet more than 3 units on any single game**
- **Never bet more than 5 games per day**
- **Never bet a team you can't justify with data**
- **Never chase a line that's already moved past value**
- **Never increase unit size after losses**
- **Always track every bet** (side, line, result, closing line)
- **If 3+ of the signals above conflict, pass on the game**
